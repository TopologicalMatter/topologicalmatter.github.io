A Dive Inside the Proton: Layer by Layer

If you could shrink down to a femtometer ($10^{-15}\text{ meters}$) and fly from the outside vacuum straight into the center of a proton, you wouldn't hit a hard surface. Instead, you would pass through four continuous wave zones:

ZONE 1 • r > 0.80 fm

1. Outer Wave Halo & Spin Rotor

At the outskirts, the wave untwists smoothly back into calm space. The entire knot spins quantum-mechanically, generating the proton's magnetic field ($\mu_p = 2.732\,\mu_N$) and $7.8\%$ of its mass ($73.3\text{ MeV}$).

Proton vs. Neutron: Identical 3D wave knot spinning in opposite internal directions!
ZONE 2 • r ≈ 0.55 fm (F = 90°)

2. Twisted Space Energy Shell

Halfway in, the vacuum medium is twisted perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the outside world. Like a tightly wound rubber band, this spherical shell stores $92.2\%$ of the proton's mass ($865.6\text{ MeV}$) as pure wave tension.

Traps a left-handed chiral fermion wave on the shell surface.
ZONE 3 • r ≈ 0.35–0.45 fm

3. Three $B=1/3$ "Quark" Whirlpools

Inside the shell, the single $B=1$ knot splits along three symmetry axes into three $1/3$-twist vortex cores ("merons") locked $120^\circ$ apart in an equilateral triangle and tied by a Y-shaped tension cord.

Explains why high-energy colliders resolve 3 quarks inside 1 wave!
ZONE 4 • Center r = 0 (F = 180°)

4. Cushioned Anti-Vacuum Core

At the exact center, the wave is flipped upside-down ($180^\circ$). Instead of collapsing into an infinite point singularity ($1/r \to \infty$), stiff short-range $\omega$-meson wave pressure acts like a cushion.

Energy & gravity stay smooth and finite all the way to $r = 0$.
