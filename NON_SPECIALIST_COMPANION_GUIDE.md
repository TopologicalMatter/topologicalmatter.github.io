## 1. Executive Summary: What Is the Big Picture?

Modern physics describes the universe using two different rulebooks that do not fit smoothly together:
1. **Quantum Field Theory (QFT)** treats elementary matter particles (like quarks and electrons) as *infinitely small points* of zero size moving through space. Because their size is assumed to be zero, dividing by distance $r \to 0$ creates mathematical infinities right at the center of every particle.
2. **Einstein's General Relativity (GR)** treats gravity as the smooth bending of empty spacetime geometry. When combined with point-like masses, it predicts infinite-density singularities at the centers of black holes, particles, and the Big Bang.

### How This Framework Offers a Unified Perspective
Instead of separating the world into "empty geometry" and "zero-size point particles," this research models the vacuum as a continuous, elastic, relativistic **wave medium**. In this medium, matter particles are not zero-size points—they are **stable, three-dimensional topological wave knots** (called *solitons*).

Thinking of particles as self-trapped wave knots addresses several longstanding puzzles in a single mathematical framework:
* **Why particles come in whole units:** You cannot put "half a twist" into a closed loop of rope. Topology forces wave knots to carry whole-number twists ($B = 1, 2, 3, \dots$), explaining why proton and neutron numbers (baryon number) are strictly conserved.
* **Where mass ($E = mc^2$) and realistic nuclear binding come from:** Over $92\%$ of a proton's mass comes directly from the trapped wave tension inside its finite core ($\sim 0.76 \times 10^{-15}\text{ m}$ across), without needing point particles. Moreover, when protons and neutrons bind into atomic nuclei like the deuteron ($B=2$) or helium-4 ($B=4$), short-range vector-meson repulsion ($\mathcal{L}_6$) balances two-pion attraction so nuclei bind softly ($1.98\text{ MeV}$ and $28.6\text{ MeV}$), matching experimental nuclear physics.
* **Why fractional pieces ("quarks / merons") cannot escape:** A proton knot is built from three fractional $1/3$-twist wave centers locked in an equilateral triangle. Pulling one piece away stretches a wave-tension tube whose energy grows with distance—like stretching an unbreakable rubber band—while an isolated $1/3$ piece oscillates above the vacuum cutoff frequency and rapidly radiates away into ripples.
* **Why gravity bends light and radiates gravitational waves:** The concentrated wave energy of a soliton polarizes and stretches the surrounding vacuum medium. Near a massive body, this changes the vacuum's effective refractive index and elastic frame, slowing down wave clocks and contracting wave rulers—reproducing Einstein's light bending, planetary orbit precession, spinning frame-dragging, and gravitational waves, while keeping gravity completely finite at $r = 0$.
* **How the universe expands without a Big Bang singularity, a vacuum-energy catastrophe, or mystery dark-matter particles:** On cosmic scales, the uniform dielectric state of the vacuum medium evolves over time ($a(t)$). Because proton and atom wave knots are held at a fixed size by their internal wave stiffness, they do not stretch as the universe expands—whereas free light waves traveling between galaxies stretch and redshift ($1 + z = a_0/a(t)$). Running the clock backward to the early universe, the strong short-range repulsion of overlapping soliton cores ($\mathcal{L}_6$) and spin-torsion prevents an infinite-density Big Bang singularity and triggers a **smooth cosmic bounce and early inflation**. Meanwhile, because an unconstrained wave medium in static equilibrium has zero net pressure, the naive $10^{120}$ vacuum-energy catastrophe cancels out, leaving only a tiny residual horizon energy set by the pion wave scale ($\sim 2.5\text{ meV}$) that matches observed dark energy, while topologically neutral ($B = 1 - 1 = 0$) knot–antiknot breather states formed during the early chiral phase transition act as dark matter ($\Omega_{\text{DM}}/\Omega_B \approx 5.44$).

### Guide to Section 2: Why Does a Wave Knot Need Stiffness, and Where Does It Come From?
If you make a wave packet in ordinary water or air, it quickly spreads out and disappears. On the other hand, if you have a simple nonlinear wave that pulls inward without resistance, it collapses down to an infinitely sharp point. To form a stable, finite-sized particle (a soliton), the wave medium must have both **surface tension** (which pulls the knot inward) and **short-distance bending stiffness** (which pushes outward when the knot is squeezed too tightly).

In early soliton models, physicists added higher-derivative "stiffness formulas" ($\mathcal{L}_4$ and $\mathcal{L}_6$) by hand just to prevent collapse. In Section 2 of the paper, we show how those exact stiffness terms arise naturally from standard short-range vector waves ($\rho$ and $\omega$ mesons) already known to exist in nuclear physics. Because the strengths of those vector waves are measured independently in meson experiments, the stiffness of the proton knot is tied directly to known physics rather than introduced as an arbitrary free parameter.
